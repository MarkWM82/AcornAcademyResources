Why Are Saints in Nativity Paintings?

Some people pictured in religious paintings lived centuries after Jesus was born. Artists include them to honor the saints, the community, or the people who commissioned the paintings. This does not mean those saints were actually standing beside the manger according to Luke.

Saints often have recognizable attributes: objects such as a bell, a wheel, a jar or a spear. The attributes help viewers know who is being shown.

Try to identify the saint from the object before reading the person's name. In the game, questions use close-up images to make small details easier to see.

Saint Identification Guide

Saint Catherine of Alexandria

Look for: Crown; often a spiked wheel

A later Christian saint portrayed beside the Holy Family; the wheel belongs to the tradition of her martyrdom.

Painting: Fra Angelico's Nativity at San Marco

Saint Peter Martyr

Look for: A head wound; Dominican black-and-white habit

A Dominican martyr, not the same person as the apostle Saint Peter.

Painting: Fra Angelico's Nativity at San Marco

Saint Anne

Look for: Often an older woman alongside Mary

Mary's mother according to Christian tradition; not named among the shepherds in Luke.

Painting: Stom's Raleigh Adoration of the Shepherds

Saint Thomas the Apostle

Look for: Spear

A patron saint of the commissioning family; the spear recalls martyrdom traditions.

Painting: Portinari Triptych, left panel

Saint Anthony the Abbot

Look for: Bell and staff

An early hermit saint, distinct from Saint Anthony of Padua.

Painting: Portinari Triptych, left panel

Saint Margaret of Antioch

Look for: Dragon

The dragon appears in her legend and helps identify her.

Painting: Portinari Triptych, right panel

Mary Magdalene

Look for: Ointment jar

Her ointment jar is a frequently used attribute in Christian art.

Painting: Portinari Triptych, right panel

Saint John the Baptist

Look for: Associated with baptizing Jesus in the Jordan

A separate figure from John the Evangelist; in Gospel narratives he prepares the way for Jesus.

Painting: Ghirlandaio's Adoration of the Magi

Saint John the Evangelist

Look for: Traditionally associated with the fourth Gospel

A different saint from John the Baptist; the two have different lives and artistic attributes.

Painting: Ghirlandaio's Adoration of the Magi

Look for Prophets Too

Duccio includes Isaiah and Ezekiel in narrow panels beside the Nativity. They lived long before Jesus and are connected with the story through biblical prophecy, rather than being visitors at the birth.

Distinguishing prophets, saints, shepherds and Magi is an important part of recognizing sacred art.
