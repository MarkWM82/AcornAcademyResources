Learn to Identify, Not Just Memorize

The same saint can look different in artworks from different places or centuries. A name is not enough. Study recurring attributes (objects), clothing, relationships, the scene, inscriptions, and the donor's patron saint. When these clues disagree, it's better to say the identity is uncertain.

Every person here has one ongoing entry. New artworks will be added to that entry, so a child can transfer what they've learned to other paintings.

Biblical figures and angels

Mary, Joseph and Gabriel appear in the Gospel narratives and are honored in Catholic tradition. Gabriel is an archangel, not a human being.

Later saints and prophets

Artists also paint saints and Old Testament prophets alongside Gospel scenes. Their presence can be symbolic rather than a claim they witnessed an event.

Find a Person or Attribute

The Virgin Mary

Biblical

Jesus' mother. In the Annunciation, Gabriel announces that she will bear Jesus.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • Usually the woman receiving a message from an angel in Annunciation scenes
  • Often wears blue, though her clothing varies
  • Often shown with a book, a lectern, a lily nearby, or an enclosed garden

Check carefully: A blue robe or a book alone does not prove that a woman is Mary. Identify her role in the story and surrounding figures.

Artwork and story examples: Leonardo's Annunciation · Jan van Eyck's Annunciation · Fra Angelico's Prado Annunciation

Saint Gabriel the Archangel

Angel

God's messenger who announces to Mary that she will bear Jesus.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • Winged messenger addressing Mary
  • Often carries lilies, an olive branch, a staff or a scroll with a greeting
  • May kneel, bow, point toward heaven or approach Mary

Check carefully: Wings alone show that someone is an angel, not necessarily Gabriel. Use the scene and his message to identify him.

Saint Ansanus

Saint

An early Christian martyr and patron of Siena. He is included in Simone Martini and Lippo Memmi's Annunciation because the altarpiece was made for his altar.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • A standing young male martyr on the far left of the altarpiece
  • A banner showing Siena's traditional black-and-white colors
  • A martyr's palm

Check carefully: A banner or palm by itself is not unique to Ansanus. The Siena altar's dedication and the painting's history provide important supporting evidence.

Artwork and story examples: Simone Martini and Lippo Memmi's Annunciation

Saint Joseph

Biblical

Mary's husband, from the family of David, and Jesus' earthly guardian.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • Shown close to Mary and the infant Jesus in Nativity scenes
  • Often appears as an older bearded man, sometimes standing apart or holding a staff
  • In other artworks may hold a lily, tools associated with carpentry, or the Christ Child

Check carefully: Age, beard or staff is not enough on its own. Identify the Holy Family scene and Joseph's relationship to Mary and Jesus.

Artwork and story examples: The Birth of Jesus · The Shepherds' Visit

Saint Anne

Saint

Mary's mother according to Christian tradition.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • Often shown as an older woman teaching the young Mary to read
  • May appear with Mary and the infant Jesus to show three generations
  • Often accompanied by a book in teaching scenes

Check carefully: An older woman near Mary is not automatically Anne. The traditional family relationship, a teaching scene or a documented painting context matters.

Artwork and story examples: Matthias Stom's Raleigh Adoration · Saint Anne guide

Saint Catherine of Alexandria

Saint

A learned Christian martyr of royal status in later tradition.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • Spiked wheel associated with her martyrdom legend
  • Crown denoting the princess of tradition
  • Sometimes a sword or a book

Check carefully: Fra Angelico's Nativity shows her crown, but a wheel may not appear. Different paintings show different attributes.

Artwork and story examples: Fra Angelico's San Marco Nativity · Learn Catherine and Peter

Saint Peter Martyr (Peter of Verona)

Saint

A thirteenth-century Dominican preacher and martyr.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • Black-and-white Dominican habit
  • A head wound or blade associated with martyrdom
  • Sometimes a martyr's palm

Check carefully: Do not confuse him with Saint Peter the Apostle, who is often shown with keys. Look for Dominican clothing and the martyr's injury.

Saint Thomas the Apostle

Saint

An apostle of Jesus, shown as a patron saint of Tommaso Portinari.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • Spear associated with traditions about his martyrdom
  • Can appear with a donor named Thomas or Tommaso
  • Sometimes shown touching Jesus' wounds in Resurrection scenes

Check carefully: Do not assume everyone holding a spear is Thomas. Combine the attribute, name saint and surrounding context.

Saint Anthony the Abbot

Saint

An Egyptian desert hermit of the third and fourth centuries.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • T-shaped tau staff or crutch
  • Bell and sometimes a pig
  • Often an elderly monk or hermit

Check carefully: Not Saint Anthony of Padua. A pig, bell or tau staff together provide stronger clues than the name Anthony alone.

Artwork and story examples: Portinari Triptych · Compare the two Anthonys

Saint Anthony of Padua

Saint

A thirteenth-century Franciscan friar, a different person from Anthony the Abbot.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • Young Franciscan friar's habit
  • Lily, book or the Christ Child

Check carefully: His objects differ from the bell, tau staff and pig often associated with Anthony the Abbot.

Saint Margaret of Antioch

Saint

A martyr associated with a dragon in her legend.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • Dragon beneath or beside her
  • Often carrying a cross or palm

Check carefully: Her identity in the Portinari Triptych is established. Do not assume that the disputed female saint in Simone Martini's Annunciation is Margaret.

Saint Mary Magdalene

Saint

A follower of Jesus, traditionally associated with anointing and a jar of ointment.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • Ointment or perfume jar
  • Sometimes loose long hair or rich garments

Check carefully: A jar is a conventional symbol, not a guaranteed unique label. Check the scene and other figures.

Where to Read About Jesus' Baptism

To learn what John the Baptist did, read Matthew 3:13–17, which describes Jesus coming to John and being baptized in the Jordan River. You can also read Mark 1:9–11 and Luke 3:21–22. These accounts help distinguish John the Baptist from John the Evangelist.

Read Matthew 3 in the approved Catholic Bible · Read Mark 1

Saint John the Baptist

Saint

The prophet who prepared the way for Jesus and baptized him in the Jordan.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • Camel-hair clothing or rough wilderness garments
  • Reed cross-staff, lamb or scroll referring to the Lamb of God
  • May appear beside a river or in a baptism scene

Check carefully: Not Saint John the Evangelist. In a scene depicting the Baptism of Jesus, the person baptizing Jesus is John the Baptist.

Artwork and story examples: Ghirlandaio's Innocenti altarpiece · The two Johns

Saint John the Evangelist

Saint

Traditionally associated with the fourth Gospel and the eagle symbol.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • Book or writing materials
  • An eagle used as the Evangelist symbol
  • Sometimes a youthful apostle with a cup or chalice

Check carefully: Not John the Baptist. Book and eagle are stronger clues than simply being a young man.

Artwork and story examples: Ghirlandaio's Innocenti altarpiece · The two Johns

Isaiah

Prophet

An Old Testament prophet whose words Christians connect with the coming of Jesus.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • Often holds a scroll or book, sometimes with a quotation from Isaiah
  • In Duccio's Nativity the left scroll quotes Isaiah 7:14

Check carefully: Scrolls are common to prophets, so the biblical quotation is the strongest identifying clue in Duccio's painting.

Ezekiel

Prophet

An Old Testament prophet whose book includes the image of a closed gate, later interpreted symbolically by Christians.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • Often holds a scroll or book
  • In Duccio's Nativity the right scroll refers to Ezekiel 44:2

Check carefully: The scroll's text matters more than which side of the panel he stands on.

Same Story, Different Artists

Compare Mary and Gabriel in two very different Annunciations. Try to identify each person from what they are doing, rather than from a particular color or position. Then check the master entries below for other symbols.

A repeated clue is more useful than a memorized position. Gabriel may hold lilies, an olive branch, or a scroll; he remains the messenger. Additional figures can be saints shown for devotional reasons, not characters Luke says were present.

New People from the Visitation

These entries connect the Visitation with earlier Annunciation and Nativity learning. Look for recurring clues across different artists, not memorized positions in one picture.

Saint Elizabeth (mother of John the Baptist)

Biblical

Mary's older relative, wife of Zechariah and mother of John the Baptist. At the Visitation, she welcomes Mary and is filled with the Holy Spirit.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • Older expectant woman welcoming a younger pregnant traveler in a Visitation scene
  • May embrace Mary, clasp her hands, or touch her pregnancy
  • May be shown near Zechariah's house and joined by her husband

Check carefully: No single color, age or position identifies Elizabeth in every work. The Visitation event, her relationship to Mary and John's pregnancy are the strongest combined clues.

Artwork and story examples: Albertinelli's Visitation · Pontormo's Visitation · Bloch's Meeting of Mary and Elizabeth · Rembrandt's Visitation

Saint Zechariah (father of John the Baptist)

Biblical

Elizabeth's husband and John's father, a priest who receives a message from Gabriel in Luke 1.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • Older man near Elizabeth's home or greeting scene in some Visitation paintings
  • Sometimes helped down steps, as in Rembrandt
  • Sometimes shown with priestly imagery or in a Temple scene

Check carefully: Beards and old age are not distinctive attributes. Identify Zechariah through the biblical situation, relationship to Elizabeth and trustworthy museum documentation.

Saint Mary Jacobi

Saint

A holy woman identified by inscription in Ghirlandaio's Louvre Visitation. Christian traditions associate her with women who followed Jesus.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • Identified by inscription at the upper left of Ghirlandaio's 1491 Louvre Visitation
  • Often depicted with other female followers of Jesus in later Christian art

Check carefully: She has no uniquely reliable dress color or object in this painting. Do not identify an unfamiliar figure as Mary Jacobi without inscriptions or stronger context.

Saint Mary Salome

Saint

A holy woman identified by inscription in Ghirlandaio's Louvre Visitation. She is distinct from Mary the mother of Jesus.

Look for these clues in different artworks:
  • Identified by the upper inscription at the right of Ghirlandaio's Louvre Visitation
  • Traditionally associated with women who followed Jesus and visited his tomb

Check carefully: Her garments and prayerful gesture in this artwork are not unique identifying symbols. Seek inscriptions, artwork context and reliable museum identifications.

Practice: Can You Recognize Them?

A winged messenger approaches Mary with a lily. Who is this most likely?

Gabriel, because the winged messenger is delivering the Annunciation to Mary. The lily is an additional clue, not a unique sign of all depictions of Gabriel.

A Dominican saint with a wound in his head. Which Peter is he?

Saint Peter Martyr (Peter of Verona), not Peter the Apostle with keys.

A monk has a bell and a T-shaped staff. Which Anthony?

Anthony the Abbot, rather than Anthony of Padua, who is often shown with a lily or the Christ Child.

An older woman teaches the young Mary with a book. Who might she be?

Saint Anne, Mary's mother according to tradition. The family relationship and teaching scene provide more evidence than age alone.

Two prophets have scrolls. How do you tell them apart?

Read their inscriptions, if available. Duccio's Isaiah panel quotes Isaiah 7:14; Ezekiel's panel quotes a text related to Ezekiel 44:2. Scrolls alone do not name the prophets.
