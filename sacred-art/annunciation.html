The Story in the Bible

According to the Gospel of Luke (1:26–38), God sends the angel Gabriel to Mary in Nazareth. Gabriel tells her she will have a son named Jesus. Mary is initially troubled by the greeting and asks how this can happen. Gabriel explains that the Holy Spirit will come upon her. Mary accepts the message.

Christians call this moment the Annunciation, meaning an announcement. It is an especially common subject in Italian religious art.

The Angel Gabriel Visits Mary

Luke 1:26–38 — Retold in simpler language. This is our own retelling, not a word-for-word Bible translation. Verse ranges help you find the same parts in a Bible.

Verses 26–27Elizabeth had been expecting a baby for nearly six months. God sent an angel named Gabriel to the town of Nazareth, in a region called Galilee. Gabriel went to visit a young woman named Mary. Mary was a virgin, and she was engaged to marry a man named Joseph. Joseph was descended from King David.

Verses 28–29Gabriel came to Mary and said, “God has shown you special kindness. God is with you!” Mary was surprised and troubled by this strange greeting. She wondered what Gabriel meant.

Verses 30–33“Don't be afraid, Mary,” Gabriel said. “God is pleased with you. You are going to have a baby boy, and you will name him Jesus. He will be great, and people will call him the Son of the Most High God. God will make him a king, just as his ancestor David was a king. He will rule over the people of Israel forever, and his kingdom will never end.”

Verses 34–35Mary asked, “How can I have a baby? I'm a virgin.” Gabriel answered, “The Holy Spirit will come to you, and God's power will be with you. That is why the baby will be holy and will be called the Son of God.”

Verses 36–37“Your relative Elizabeth is also going to have a baby, even though she is old and people thought she couldn't have children. She has already been expecting her baby for six months. Nothing is impossible for God.”

Verse 38Mary answered, “I am God's servant. Let everything happen just as you have told me.” Then Gabriel left her.

26 Now in the sixth month, the angel Gabriel was sent from God to a city of Galilee named Nazareth,

27 to a virgin pledged to be married to a man whose name was Joseph, of David’s house. The virgin’s name was Mary.

28 Having come in, the angel said to her, “Rejoice, you highly favored one! The Lord is with you. Blessed are you among women!”

29 But when she saw him, she was greatly troubled at the saying, and considered what kind of salutation this might be.

30 The angel said to her, “Don’t be afraid, Mary, for you have found favor with God.

31 Behold, you will conceive in your womb and give birth to a son, and shall name him ‘Jesus.’

32 He will be great and will be called the Son of the Most High. The Lord God will give him the throne of his father David,

33 and he will reign over the house of Jacob forever. There will be no end to his Kingdom.”

34 Mary said to the angel, “How can this be, seeing I am a virgin?”

35 The angel answered her, “The Holy Spirit will come on you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you. Therefore also the holy one who is born from you will be called the Son of God.

36 Behold, Elizabeth your relative also has conceived a son in her old age; and this is the sixth month with her who was called barren.

37 For nothing spoken by God is impossible.”

38 Mary said, “Behold, the servant of the Lord; let it be done to me according to your word.” Then the angel departed from her.

From the Bible

Gabriel's visit, the greeting, Mary's question, and her response are described in Luke.

From artistic tradition

Gabriel's wings, the lily, Mary's book, and the carefully arranged garden are visual conventions that help communicate the story; Luke does not describe these details.

A Little Art History: Leonardo's Annunciation

Leonardo da Vinci painted The Annunciation around 1472, when he was only about twenty years old. At the time, he was learning and working in Florence in the workshop of another famous artist, Andrea del Verrocchio. This is one of Leonardo's earliest known paintings. Nobody knows for certain who first asked him to paint it. It came to the Uffizi Gallery in 1867 from a church in Florence.

Leonardo was fascinated by nature, and you can already see this in his painting. Look at the plants and flowers in the garden, the folds in Gabriel's clothing, and the angel's enormous wings. The wings appear to be based on those of a real bird of prey. Leonardo also painted a distant landscape, with mountains that seem to fade into the mist. Unlike some earlier religious paintings, which used golden backgrounds, Leonardo placed this biblical story in a natural-looking world.

There is something curious about this painting. Look carefully at Mary's right arm: it seems unusually long. Was this a mistake? Some art historians think Leonardo expected people to look at the painting from below and to the right, perhaps when it was displayed above a church altar. That might help explain the unusual proportions, although nobody knows for certain. Leonardo was experimenting with perspective, the technique artists use to make a flat painting appear three-dimensional. Can you find other places where he used perspective?
