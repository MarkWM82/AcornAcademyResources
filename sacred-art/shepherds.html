The Bible Story — Retold

An original simplified retelling, not an official Bible translation. Read Luke 2:8–20 for the Gospel wording.

That night, shepherds were in the countryside watching their flocks. Suddenly an angel of the Lord appeared to them, and the glory of the Lord shone around them. The shepherds were frightened.

The angel told them not to be afraid. This was good news that would bring great joy: a Savior had been born in Bethlehem. The angel gave them a sign. They would find a baby wrapped in cloths and lying in a manger.

Then a great company of heavenly beings praised God and spoke of glory in heaven and peace on earth. When the angels had gone, the shepherds decided to go to Bethlehem and see what had happened.

They went quickly and found Mary, Joseph and the baby lying in the manger. The shepherds told others what they had heard about the child. Everyone who heard their report was amazed. Mary treasured these things and thought about them. The shepherds returned to their flocks, praising God.

Artists often show the shepherds bowing or kneeling beside Jesus, sometimes with sheep or lanterns. They may use bright light shining from the child to suggest Jesus as the light of the world. Luke tells us about the shepherds and the manger but does not say that the child literally glowed.

Some devotional paintings include additional saints. In Matthias Stom's painting at Raleigh, a woman beside Mary is identified as Saint Anne, Mary's mother according to Christian tradition. In Hugo van der Goes's Portinari Triptych, a family and its patron saints appear in side panels. These figures help us learn about saints, but we should not confuse them with Luke's shepherds.

Different artists may combine events or add symbols, prophets and later saints. We can appreciate those choices while noticing what the Gospel actually says.

Why Does Jesus Seem to Shine?

In both Correggio's and Matthias Stom's paintings, the newborn Jesus appears to send out light that illuminates the faces nearby. The Gospel of Luke does not say that the baby's body literally glowed. The painters use light as religious symbolism.

Christians use the image of Jesus as light for the world: a way of talking about hope, goodness and God's presence in human life. The artists make this idea visible. Ask yourself: how would the meaning change if the room were lit by an ordinary lantern instead?

Stom's painting also includes Saint Anne, Mary's mother according to tradition. Her presence is supported by the North Carolina Museum of Art's description. Learn why age alone is not enough to recognize Anne in other paintings in the saint guide.

Correggio — Adoration of the Shepherds (Holy Night)

c. 1528–1530Story and symbols

Jesus seems to be the source of light, illuminating Mary and the shepherds. This represents the Christian idea of Christ as light.

A Little Art History

Correggio creates a nighttime scene where light does the storytelling. The infant Jesus appears brighter than the surrounding room.

The shepherds react to the child with amazement. Their ordinary clothing contrasts with the importance of the event.

The glow is a symbolic artistic choice, not a statement in Luke that the baby literally shone in the room.

Matthias Stom — Adoration of the Shepherds (Raleigh)

c. 1635–16371 identified saints/prophets

The museum identifies an older woman beside Mary as Saint Anne, Mary's mother in Christian tradition. Luke does not list Anne among the shepherds.

A Little Art History

Matthias Stom, a Dutch painter working in Italy, made this painting in the seventeenth century. Like other artists influenced by Caravaggio, he used strong contrasts of light and shadow.

The faces of the visitors are lit by the infant Jesus, who draws everyone's gaze toward the center.

Alongside Mary, Joseph and the shepherds, the North Carolina Museum of Art identifies Mary's mother, Saint Anne. She is a figure of later Christian tradition, not named as a visitor in Luke.

Hugo van der Goes — Portinari Triptych

c. 1473–14784 identified saints/prophets

Four patron saints accompany the donor family. Look for Thomas' spear, Anthony's bell, Margaret's dragon and Mary Magdalene's ointment jar.

A Little Art History

The Portinari Triptych was painted in Flanders for a Florentine banking family and eventually arrived in Florence, where its naturalism impressed other artists.

The large center panel shows the shepherds approaching Jesus. The smaller side panels are different: members of the Portinari family kneel with their patron saints.

Saint Thomas holds a spear; Saint Anthony the Abbot has a bell; Saint Margaret appears with a dragon; and Mary Magdalene carries ointment. These attributes tell us who the saints are even without their names written beside them.
