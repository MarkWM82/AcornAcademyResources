Learn what an attribute means

An attribute is an object, color, gesture, animal or piece of clothing that artists often associate with a particular person or story. It may tell you about the person's life, a legend, their martyrdom, or a spiritual idea.

To interpret a painting, ask four questions: What is the clue? What does it mean? Does it recur in other paintings? What other evidence supports the identification?

An attribute can be a strong clue without being an infallible rule. Not every occurrence of blue means Mary, not every wheel indicates Catherine, and a halo does not tell us which saint we are seeing.

Why does Mary so often wear blue and red?

Blue mantle

A familiar Western artistic convention. Blue can suggest heaven, purity and Mary's elevated spiritual status. Ultramarine pigment could be exceptionally costly, making the blue a sign of the dignity and honor accorded to Mary.

Red or rose garment

Red is frequently interpreted as love, maternal devotion and human feeling. In some religious interpretations it also anticipates the suffering Mary will witness during Jesus' Passion.

The two colors together help us notice Mary in many Western Annunciation, Nativity and Visitation paintings. They are not a universal dress code. The exact interpretations vary by time, place and artist. Blue can have practical and patronage meanings as well as theological ones.

One useful exception is Nicolas Poussin's Annunciation, where Mary's cloak is yellow; the National Gallery interprets the color as hope and purity. In other traditions, artists arrange blue and red differently. This is why we combine clothing with the person's role and surrounding figures.

Symbols and their traditional meanings

Mary: colors and recurring symbols

Blue mantle

Common meaning: Heaven, Marian dignity and purity; blue also became associated with costly ultramarine pigment.

How to recognize it: Look for a blue outer mantle as a common supporting clue to Mary.

Remember: Not every Mary is blue, and other saints can wear blue.

Red or rose-colored tunic

Common meaning: Love, maternal devotion and, in some readings, the future suffering of Christ.

How to recognize it: Look for red or rose beneath Maryundefineds often-blue mantle.

Remember: Meaning varies by painting and period; not a universal red=humanity formula.

White lily

Common meaning: Purity, especially in Annunciation imagery.

How to recognize it: Gabriel may present lilies, or lilies may stand in a vase near Mary.

Remember: Lilies appear with other saints and are not a name tag on their own.

Halo or gold surrounding the head

How to recognize it: A wheel beside a crowned woman may suggest Saint Catherine.

Practice reading the clues

Mary wears a blue mantle in one picture and yellow in another. Is one picture necessarily wrong?

No. Artists may use different materials or symbolic traditions. The blue cloak is common but not obligatory. Combine colors with her role and the Gospel story.

A woman carries a spiked wheel and a crown. What could the objects mean?

They suggest Saint Catherine of Alexandria: the wheel recalls her martyrdom legend, and the crown recalls her traditional royal background. Compare other paintings for confirmation.

A young man has camel hair and a reed cross. What is the connection?

These are familiar attributes of John the Baptist, who lived in the wilderness and prepared the way for Jesus.

Why is an ointment jar a clue to Mary Magdalene?

It recalls Christian traditions about anointing Jesus. Because several biblical women were historically conflated in art, we should also examine the scene and its history.
